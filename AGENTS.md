# Spanish writing evaluation

## Status and purpose

This rubric is proposed for John's review. Do not add an article evaluation until
John agrees to the rubric. Once agreed, use it for requested evaluations in this
repository.

Help John develop accurate, idiomatic Spanish while preserving his intended
meaning, voice, humor, and register. Read the writing and its stated intentions
before evaluating it. Do not replace his voice with a generic formal style.

## Separate three kinds of feedback

Label each finding explicitly:

1. **Error — correction needed.** A problem with spelling, accent marks,
   punctuation, agreement, conjugation, syntax, required prepositions, or word
   meaning. Explain the specific problem and give the smallest useful correction.
2. **Naturalness — idiomatic revision recommended.** A construction may be
   interpretable or grammatically possible but would sound unusual or convey the
   wrong nuance in the intended context. Explain why and suggest an idiomatic
   formulation. Distinguish a strong non-native construction from a merely less
   common choice. Do not claim that no native speaker would ever say something.
3. **Alternative — optional.** The original is acceptable. Offer a synonym,
   different expression, or stylistic variation for exploration, explicitly
   stating that it is not a correction. Explain changes in meaning, emphasis,
   register, or regional usage; do not present near-synonyms as interchangeable.

When a passage has both an error and an optional stylistic opportunity, separate
the minimal correction from the optional rewrite. Do not inflate preferences into
errors or bury corrections among lists of synonyms.

## Evaluation method

- Evaluate the requested version; default to `borrador.md` when no version is
  specified. Include its title. Read `comentarios.md` for intent and practice goals,
  but do not silently expand the review to correcting John's commentary.
- Review the entire text for errors and conspicuously unnatural phrasing, not
  only the expressions selected for practice. Group repeated patterns and identify
  their occurrences rather than repeating the same explanation unnecessarily.
- Quote the relevant original wording, label the finding, give a suggested Spanish
  formulation, and explain the reason in English unless John requests Spanish.
  Use enough surrounding context to make each finding easy to locate.
- Review each expression John intended to practice: whether its meaning,
  construction, and register fit this particular context. Note expressions used
  successfully as well as those needing work. Note a listed expression that does
  not appear in the text without treating its absence as a language error.
- Give optional alternatives selectively, especially for the target expressions.
  Use short Spanish examples when they help distinguish usage.
- Respect established regional varieties of Spanish. Identify regional preferences
  when relevant; do not treat valid Latin American or Peninsular usage as an error.
  State uncertainty and consult reliable linguistic sources when needed, rather
  than asserting a doubtful rule or an unsupported frequency claim.
- If the intended meaning is ambiguous, state the interpretation behind the
  suggestion and offer conditional readings or ask for clarification. Do not
  silently change the claim, degree of certainty, or tone.
- Close with a few concrete priorities for the next piece. Do not assign a numeric
  score or CEFR level unless requested. Do not produce a full rewritten version
  unless requested.

## Where feedback belongs

- Put requested feedback beneath `## Evaluación de IA (Codex)` in the writing's
  `comentarios.md` and identify it as generated by Codex.
- Organize it into errors, naturalness, optional alternatives, and practice
  priorities. Clearly label successful uses in the target-expression discussion.
  Cross-reference earlier corrections instead of repeating them.
- Preserve John's draft, final version, intentions, and other commentary. Do not
  edit them, rename their directory, or create a final version unless requested.
- Do not overwrite a previous evaluation without authorization. If John edits or
  mixes feedback into his own commentary, preserve explicit attribution for any
  retained Codex-generated sections.
