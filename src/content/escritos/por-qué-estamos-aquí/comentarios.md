### Errores — correcciones necesarias

These are corrections, not optional synonyms. They follow the order of the draft.

1. **“Pórque estamos aquí” → “Por qué estamos aquí”.** For the intended meaning “Why we are here,” use two words and put the accent on *qué*. As an explanatory heading, it can stand without question marks; as a direct question, write “¿Por qué estamos aquí?”. If you intended “Because we are here,” that would instead be “Porque estamos aquí,” but that does not seem to be the intended title. See [FundéuRAE on *por qué* and *porqué*](https://www.fundeu.es/consulta/por-que-y-porque-951/).

2. **“¿Me estás bromeando?” → “¿Estás bromeando?”.** *Bromear* does not take *me* in this construction. If the intended sense is specifically “Are you kidding me?”, “¿Me estás tomando el pelo?” is an idiomatic alternative, with a stronger suggestion of teasing or deception.

3. **“estoy cansado de ser en…” → “estoy cansado de estar en…”.** A situation you find yourself in takes *estar* here. *Cansado de* itself is correct.

4. **“Dejame explicar” → “Déjame explicar”.** The imperative with the attached pronoun requires the accent. The more self-contained formulation “Déjame explicarlo” explicitly supplies what is being explained; that addition is a naturalness improvement rather than the accent correction itself.

5. **“La ortografía es mas sencilla de inglés” → “La ortografía es más sencilla que la del inglés”.** Comparative *más* needs an accent; the comparison uses *que*. *La del inglés* stands for *la ortografía del inglés*, so the comparison is between spelling systems.

6. **“puedes alzar a un nivel básico” → “puedes alcanzar un nivel básico”.** The verb needed for attaining a level is *alcanzar*, with a direct object. *Alzar* does not express that meaning in this construction. “Puedes llegar a un nivel básico” also works, with *a*.

7. **“ahí puedes restar por años” → “ahí puedes quedarte por años”.** *Restar* is not the verb for remaining in a state here. *Quedarte* preserves the image of staying stuck. *Por años* is acceptable; “durante años” is an optional alternative, not a necessary correction.

8. **“una criatura medio-gringo desatado” → “una criatura medio gringa desatada”.** With *criatura* as the head noun, the adjectives take feminine agreement; adverbial *medio* stays unchanged and takes no hyphen. However, this minimal repair still leaves a meaning question: *medio gringa* suggests “somewhat/half gringo,” not specifically a learner with incomplete Spanish. See the naturalness discussion below. [RAE/ASALE explains invariable adverbial *medio*](https://temporalweb.asale.org/dpd/medio).

9. **“Desde poco tiempo tomé la decisión” → “Hace poco tiempo tomé la decisión”.** A completed decision a short time ago calls for *hace poco (tiempo)*. For an ongoing situation, use “Desde hace poco estudio…”. Those express different time relationships.

10. **“Puedo hacer frentes con tales conversaciones” → “Puedo hacer frente a tales conversaciones”.** The expression is *hacer frente a*: singular *frente*, followed by *a*. This repairs the construction; whether the expression is the best fit is a separate naturalness question below.

11. **“mi facilidad de expresión ha estancado” → “mi facilidad de expresión se ha estancado”.** Becoming stagnant uses pronominal *estancarse*. Without *se*, transitive *estancar* normally needs something being held back.

12. **“o resultó limitado a un nivel fijo” → “o ha quedado limitada a un nivel fijo”.** *Limitada* agrees with feminine *facilidad*. “Ha quedado limitada” also maintains the present-relevant time frame of “se ha estancado.” The shift to *resultó* is not inherently ungrammatical, but it does not express the apparent current, continuing limitation as clearly. If you mean the two formulations to explain the same condition, *o* is possible; use *y* only if you intend two separate claims.

13. **“un programa serio de lectura y escuchando” → “un programa serio de lectura y escucha”.** Coordinate two nouns after *de*. The gerund *escuchando* does not work as an English-style activity noun in this structure.

14. **“otros recursos que se puede importar” → “otros recursos que se pueden importar”.** In the straightforward passive-*se* reading, plural *recursos* requires *pueden*. “Otros recursos que puedo importar” is another clear formulation if you mean your own ability to import them. Some impersonal-*se* patterns vary regionally; the agreeing version avoids that complication in edited prose.

15. **“un app” → “una app”.** The noun is feminine, following *aplicación*. “Una aplicación” also avoids the borrowing. See [Fundéu Guzmán Ariza on the gender of *app*](https://fundeu.do/app/).

16. **“creer en si y sigue escuchando” → “creer en sí y seguir escuchando”.** Reflexive *sí* takes an accent. *Tiene que* governs both coordinated infinitives: “tiene que creer… y seguir…”. *Sigue* would be a finite verb or an imperative, which breaks that construction. “Creer en sí mismo” makes the reflexive meaning more explicit.

17. **“Esta idea restar importancia de hábitos de producción” → “Esta idea resta importancia a los hábitos de producción”.** The sentence needs finite *resta*. The expression is *restar importancia a algo*, not *de algo*. The article *los* is appropriate for these habits considered as a category. The [Diccionario del español de México](https://dem.colmex.mx/ver/restar) documents this construction.

18. **“Se aprende escuchar escuchando” → “Se aprende a escuchar escuchando”.** The construction for acquiring an ability is *aprender a + infinitivo*. The repeated verb is effective here and does not need to be removed.

19. **“pero cuantas veces has oído una persona decir…” → “pero ¿cuántas veces has oído a una persona decir…?”.** The direct rhetorical question needs both question marks and an accent on *cuántas*. Use personal *a* before *una persona* in this construction. One possible punctuation of the entire ending is: `pero ¿cuántas veces has oído a una persona decir: «Pues lo puedo entender, pero no lo puedo hablar»?` The quoted sentence does not take a full stop inside the surrounding question.

20. **“es nada menos con un sistema integral de aprender español” → “es nada menos que un sistema integral para aprender español”.** The emphatic expression is *nada menos que*. *Para aprender* expresses the system's purpose; *de aprender* does not fit this noun phrase as written.

21. **“con confianza y todas esperanzas de éxito” → “con confianza y todas las esperanzas de éxito”.** This use of *todas* requires the definite article. The result is grammatical, though its naturalness is discussed below.

22. **“sin ningunos atajos” → “sin ningún atajo” or “sin atajos”.** For this ordinary count-noun use, avoid plural *ningunos*. The simpler “sin atajos” preserves your emphasis well.

23. **“Solo así que podemos llevarlo a cabo” → “Solo así podemos llevarlo a cabo”.** *Solo así* directly modifies the clause; it does not take *que*. *Así que* is a different connective meaning roughly “so/therefore.” *Podemos* is fine for present possibility. “Podremos” would optionally emphasize future success.

### Naturalidad — revisiones idiomáticas recomendadas

These points concern meaning, collocation, or the effect on a reader, rather than additional spelling rules.

- **“Con mi historial, creo que al mundo debo una explicación”.** Understandable, but “creo que le debo una explicación al mundo” is a more idiomatic conversational ordering. The fronted *al mundo* can work as deliberate emphasis; it is not simply a grammar error. *Con mi historial* successfully preserves the joke.

- **“el ‘infierno intermedio’”.** The metaphor is intelligible, but does not by itself identify an established learning term. If you mean the familiar plateau phenomenon, introduce it as “la meseta del nivel intermedio” or “ese ‘infierno’ del nivel intermedio.” The latter retains your exaggerated tone. This is about making the image clear, not forbidding an invented metaphor.

- **“una criatura… desatada en un mundo desprevenido”.** *Desatada* suggests being uninhibited or out of control. If your intended joke is “a creature unleashed on an unsuspecting world,” “un gringo con un español a medio hacer, suelto en un mundo desprevenido” is a possible comic formulation. This is a larger optional rewrite conditioned on that reading, not a minimal correction. The deliberately dramatic *mundo desprevenido* can stay.

- **“marcar un rumbo fuera de este estado intermedio”.** *Marcar un rumbo* fits your intended metaphor, but attaching *fuera de* makes the course and destination relationship awkward. “Marcar un rumbo para salir de este estado intermedio” states the purpose clearly. Keep the expression you are practicing.

- **“hacer frente a tales conversaciones”.** Once corrected, it is grammatical, but frames the conversations as difficulties to confront. If you mean simply “I can manage in these conversations,” “Puedo desenvolverme en esas conversaciones” better matches that meaning. “Puedo defenderme en esas conversaciones” adds a modest, conversational sense of getting by. Keep *hacer frente a* if the challenge is intentional.

- **“mi facilidad de expresión”.** Possible Spanish, but if you specifically mean the ability to speak smoothly, “mi fluidez al hablar” is more precise. *Facilidad de expresión* can include ease or skill in expressing ideas more broadly.

- **“un ejemplar excelente del método de ‘entrada (información) comprensible’”.** For an illustrative instance, use *ejemplo*, rather than *ejemplar*, which suggests a specimen, individual copy, or member of a class. Also, an app can provide material without itself being an example of a method. If that is your intention: “una aplicación excelente para aprender mediante input comprensible.” *Input comprensible* is recognizable terminology in language-learning discussion; for readers unfamiliar with it, explain what it means. This recommendation does not endorse any particular learning theory.

- **“los reclamos de marketing que prometen…”.** *Reclamos* can legitimately mean advertising appeals, so this is not an automatic false-friend error. But if you mean the assertions being made rather than attention-getting advertising, “las promesas publicitarias de que…” is clearer. “Plantar cara a las promesas publicitarias…” retains a combative tone; see the optional alternative below for a less confrontational one.

- **“Producir naturalmente como resultado de leer”.** In this context, *naturalmente* could mean either spontaneously or idiomatically. If you mean that output supposedly emerges without deliberate practice, write “Que la producción surja por sí sola como resultado de la lectura…”. If you instead mean producing natural-sounding Spanish, “Expresarse con naturalidad como resultado de leer…” says that. The choice depends on your claim.

- **“solo producir nos permite recoger los frutos de producir”.** The metaphor is appropriate, but repeated *producir* leaves the intended payoff unspecified. “Solo la práctica de la producción nos permite recoger sus frutos” keeps the claim and the expression while reducing the repetition. Whether the exclusivity of *solo* is justified is an argument to consider, not a Spanish error.

- **“la hoja de ruta… es… un sistema integral”.** Both noun phrases work, but a roadmap is a plan, while a system is a set of methods or practices. If you mean a roadmap for establishing that system, say “la hoja de ruta que propongo para poner en práctica un sistema integral…”. If you mean the system itself, make *el sistema que propongo* the subject. This is a conceptual distinction, not a ban on the metaphor.

- **“Con confianza y todas las esperanzas de éxito, disfrutando el paisaje…”.** Even after correcting the article, *todas las esperanzas de éxito* sounds less idiomatic here than “con confianza y muchas esperanzas de éxito.” The whole passage is also a sentence fragment: that can be a deliberate rhetorical flourish, but it does not explicitly attach to a main verb. If unintended, “Avanzaremos con confianza y muchas esperanzas de éxito, disfrutando del paisaje, pero sin atajos” supplies one. *Disfrutando el paisaje* is valid Spanish; *disfrutando del paisaje* is an optional variant, not a correction.

- **“llevarlo a cabo”.** Correct construction, but *lo* could refer to the system, the learning process, or the proposed plan. If you mean the roadmap specifically, that is feminine: “llevarla a cabo.” If you mean implementing the plan, explicitly naming *el plan* once would make the existing *lo* easier to follow. This is a referent question, not an automatic gender error.

### Prioridades para el próximo escrito

1. **Learn constructions as complete units:** *aprender a*, *hacer frente a*, *restar importancia a*, *nada menos que*. Your expression choices often fit the argument; the connecting words are a recurring source of errors.
2. **Check each verb chain:** finite verb where a sentence needs one (*esta idea resta*), infinitive after *tener que* (*seguir*), and the pronoun in *estancarse* (*se ha estancado*). Also check parallel nouns: *lectura y escucha*.
3. **Do a separate agreement and accent pass:** *la app*, *la facilidad… limitada*, and *déjame, más, sí, cuántas, por qué*. Separating this pass from composing lets you keep your attention on meaning while drafting.
4. **Keep practicing the metaphors in your own contexts.** *Sobre el papel*, *plantar cara a*, and *recoger los frutos* already contribute to your argument. Aim next for precise surrounding constructions and clear referents, rather than adding more expressions per paragraph.
